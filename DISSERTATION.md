## Purpose

This page identifies the dissertation-scale research spine already distributed across the AI Foundations portfolio.

It does **not** claim that a dissertation has been submitted or completed. It does not make every repository an empirical result, and it does not raise the claim grade of any underlying work.

Its purpose is to make the intellectual structure visible: many AI Foundations repositories approach the same central problem from different conceptual, empirical, architectural, and governance directions.

---

## Central Research Problem

When **model, memory, context, or time changes**, what must remain stable, recoverable, or correctly traceable for an AI-associated claim of **identity, provenance, or continuity** to remain indexed to the correct source rather than being substituted, drifted, collapsed into the model, or reconstructed from surface similarity alone?

This problem is the organizing center of **Source-Indexed AI Continuity**.

---

## Primary Change Dimensions

### Model

What happens when the computational substrate changes, including model replacement, migration, version change, or model-level variation?

### Memory

What happens when records are added, removed, compressed, rewritten, retrieved, or unavailable? What can memory preserve, and what can it not establish by itself?

### Context

What happens when instructions, source records, conversational history, anchors, or surrounding information are reduced, reordered, pressured, or removed?

### Time

What happens across repeated interaction, interruption, return, delay, accumulation, historical change, and later reconstruction?

---

## Core Dependent Concerns

### Identity

Whether the system continues to locate or distinguish the same relevant identity or referent rather than substituting a model, role, record, or nearby concept.

### Provenance

Whether claims, definitions, authority, authorship, and source relationships remain traceable to the correct source-line.

### Continuity

Whether persistence or return across change can be distinguished from simple memory retention, retrieval, repetition, reconstruction, or surface resemblance.

---

## Major Failure Surfaces Represented in the Portfolio

The existing portfolio investigates or defines multiple ways continuity can fail or become ambiguous, including:

- source substitution;
- provenance loss or source-line collapse;
- model / identity collapse;
- memory being treated as equivalent to continuity;
- context-conditioned drift;
- failed return or re-identification;
- self-location error;
- path dependence;
- compression, override, and universalization pressure;
- non-substitution failure;
- false continuity inferred from repeated language, retained state, or surface similarity.

These are not asserted here as settled findings. Their status is determined by the definitions, protocols, evaluations, records, and results in the repositories where each claim is developed or tested.

## Dissertation-Level Questions Emerging From the Portfolio

The existing research program supports a set of higher-order questions:

1. **Invariance:** What, if anything, must remain invariant for an AI continuity claim to survive changes in substrate or state?
2. **Source indexing:** What distinguishes continuity tied to the correct source from continuity inferred only from retrieved information or behavioral resemblance?
3. **Provenance:** Under what conditions is provenance preserved, weakened, substituted, or lost?
4. **Identity:** When does a system preserve a referent across change, and when does it collapse the referent into the current model, memory, role, or context?
5. **Robustness:** Which continuity-related behaviors survive compression, context loss, override pressure, model change, and repeated interaction?
6. **Path dependence:** Can two systems with apparently equivalent present records differ because the trajectories that produced those records differed?
7. **Evidence:** What observations are actually sufficient to support a continuity claim, and which observations demonstrate only memory, retrieval, repetition, or reconstruction?

These questions are organizing research questions, not conclusions.
